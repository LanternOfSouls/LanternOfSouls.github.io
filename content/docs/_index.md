# Game Design Overview

## Game-Wide Design Statement

The game is designed to evoke fear and dread through its enemies and environment. Despite this, players should have the tools, encouragement and environmental readability to rely on stealth, particularly positional stealth, to navigate levels and avoid enemies. The game should be difficult and scary, but players should come away thinking, “I wish I had done X,” rather than, “The game should have given me X.” Players should feel intrigued and motivated to keep trying, not discouraged by an unnecessarily steep initial difficulty curve.

## Fantasy Statement

You play as a Soul Master navigating a dark underworld to guide a soul to ascension. Your power comes from your experience in this world. Using your environment and the soul's light you will outwit massive, terrifying lost souls.

## Core Emotions

- High-stakes fear
- A strong protective drive toward the soul
- The player should feel aware of their environment and abilities and be able to use them to overcome enemies
- Intense relief when you reach safety

## Core Pillars

### Horror: “Light in the dark”

Players should be cautious while they play. At every skill level, they should always feel scared; they should never feel safe.

### Anticipation

Fear builds well before the player is about to die. The moment when you hear something in the woods.

- Ambiance leads into the idea that players are in a dangerous situation (lighting, sound, music, environmental cues).
- Sounds of the environment lead the player to stay in a state of anticipation (“What was that? Was that my killer?”).
- Partial information keeps the player imagining what could happen (the player can hear the enemy but not see it).
- New situations keep the player slightly unfamiliar, erring on the side of caution (for example, different rooms and varied enemy behavior).

### The player is the prey, and the enemies are the predators

- **Fear of an intelligent killer**
- **Threatening presence:** Unsettling to look at, watch and listen to.
- **Semi-omnipresence:** Enemies feel like they can come from anywhere (“Is it behind me?”).
- **Semi-omniscience:** Enemies have behaviors the player can’t explain. For example:
  - Long holds where an enemy stares at the spot where the player is hiding, even though they shouldn’t know the player is there
  - Peeks into rooms
- Enemies are unpredictable in what order they do things or what path they take, but purposeful in why they do the things they do.
- Enemies act intelligently as they patrol, investigate and wind up to kill the player.
- Enemies use pauses and stillness, not just aggression.

### Stealth

Special emphasis on player agency and interconnected level design. We want the player to feel scared, but capable as they outsmart the enemies and navigate the level.

### Single Player Companion Game

The story is focused on the relationship with the two characters. The side character is helpful, and the player should feel attached and protective of them.

## Acceptance Criteria

Because of the difficult nature of the game, as well as the different aptitudes players might have within this specific genre and with games in general, we have decided to split players into four main player behaviors and create acceptance criteria based on those behaviors.

### Player Behaviors

#### Reckless

- **Mindset:** These players do not know or care that they are in a stealth game.
- **Visibility:** Stays in enemy line of sight for long periods, not hiding and opting to run around to try to avoid enemies.
- **Noise:** Makes a lot of noise close to enemies (for example, running next to them).
- **Tool use:** Does not use tools.
- **Outcome:** Is detected quickly and often, and rarely changes behavior afterward.

#### Novice

- **Mindset:** Understands that enemies are dangerous, but makes poor decisions when trying to avoid them.
- **Visibility:** Hides occasionally, but misjudges what enemies can see, so often hides in the wrong place or at the wrong time.
- **Noise:** Tries to reduce noise by walking or crouching when enemies are close; however, they don’t understand the extent of the enemies’ hearing, so they often die because they were too loud.
- **Tool use:** Uses tools infrequently because they don’t understand the full extent of what the tools can do.
- **Outcome:** Dies frequently while learning how enemy perception and toolkit work.

#### Standard

- **Mindset:** Understands the dangers of the game and acts accordingly most of the time. This is the expected gameplay.
- **Visibility:** Notices when enemies are close and limits their visibility by reading enemy awareness, staying out of harm’s way in most situations.
- **Noise:** Manages noise when enemies are nearby, either by being stationary or crouching when an enemy is close.
- **Tool use:** Uses all tools in expected situations. For example, hiding by default even if they aren’t sure an enemy is nearby but might have suspicions; using distractions to redirect enemy attention; and using invisibility to find cover. However, they aren’t perfect at it.
- **Outcome:** Is occasionally detected and/or killed, but learns from each mistake to make more informed decisions next time.

#### Expert

- **Mindset:** Not specified yet.
- **Experience:** Is well versed in horror stealth games, either others or this one, and already has previous knowledge mastering stealth gameplay.
- **Outcome:** Is killed 1 or 2 times during the game but is still challenged.
